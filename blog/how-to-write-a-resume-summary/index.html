How to Write a Resume Summary with Evidence

Updated October 8, 2026 · JobFace product guide

Direct answer: Describe the role you are targeting, the relevant work you have actually done and one or two skills you can demonstrate. Leave out unsupported praise and invented numbers. In JobFace, the summary is optional.

Use a simple drafting framework

Start with this working pattern: “[Role or background] with experience in [relevant work]. Used [skill or tool] to [specific contribution]. Interested in [type of role, when useful].” It is a drafting aid, not a formula that every employer expects. Read the result aloud and remove anything already obvious from the title immediately above it.

Before and after

Vague fictional draft: “Highly motivated professional with excellent skills seeking a challenging position.”

More specific fictional draft: “Customer support assistant with experience organizing order questions and maintaining help notes. Updated a delivery checklist and helped two new colleagues learn the order lookup process.”

The second version gives a reader something to ask about. It is only suitable if those actions are true. Do not copy the number, job title or contribution into your own application without evidence.

If you are early in your career

Use a relevant project, placement or volunteer role rather than pretending to have professional experience. For example: “Computing student who built a room-booking project using a database and web forms. Tested duplicate-booking cases and documented the setup for classmates.” This fictional example describes work rather than asserting expertise across an entire industry.

Adapt it to a real vacancy

The US CareerOneStop: analyzing job postings recommends examining a posting’s qualifications, skills and experience requirements. For your summary, choose only the relevant requirements you can support. If the role mentions spreadsheet reporting and you have maintained a project tracker, explain that work accurately. Do not claim financial modeling because you once opened a spreadsheet.

When to omit a summary

If your draft repeats the rest of the resume without adding useful context, a shorter document may be clearer. The JobFace builder leaves the summary section out when it is empty. This is a product behavior, not advice that every applicant must omit or include a summary.

Editing checklist

  1. Underline every claim about skill, seniority or results.
  2. For each claim, identify a project or role that supports it.
  3. Delete adjectives that do not add evidence.
  4. Use the vacancy’s wording only when it accurately matches your experience.
  5. Check that the summary and experience section tell the same story.
  6. Read the downloaded resume, not just the form, before sending.
