How to Write a Resume Summary That Gets Read

A resume summary is a short opening statement that tells a recruiter what role you want, what evidence you bring and how you can contribute. It is not a list of vague adjectives.

Use a three-part structure

  1. Target: name the role or function you are pursuing.
  2. Evidence: mention one or two relevant skills, projects or measurable outcomes.
  3. Direction: explain the type of problem or team where you can contribute next.

Keep the summary specific

Replace “hard-working team player” with evidence such as “built a booking API used by 3,000 students” or “organized customer research that improved onboarding decisions.” Use the language of the job description only when it accurately describes your experience.

Example

Product intern with experience in user research, competitor analysis and prototype testing. Looking to help a consumer product team turn customer evidence into clearer decisions.
